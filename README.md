### Project Description

This capstone project applies **Geographic Information Systems (GIS), spatial analysis, and remote sensing techniques** to assess infrastructure accessibility and urban growth in Kigali, Rwanda. The project consists of two complementary analyses designed to demonstrate practical skills in geospatial data processing, spatial modelling, map production, and satellite-image analysis.

The first part focuses on identifying **underserved areas based on access to key infrastructure** within a selected district of Kigali Province. Buffer zones are created around schools, health facilities, trade centres, and national roads using specified distances of 1 km, 5 km, 3 km, and 500 m respectively. The infrastructure and buffer layers are reprojected to **UTM Zone 35S (EPSG:32735)**, dissolved, and represented using clear and distinct symbology. Areas that fall outside all four infrastructure buffer zones are identified and highlighted as potentially underserved areas. The final map layout includes essential cartographic elements such as a title, legend, scale bar, north arrow, and author information.

The second part investigates **built-up area expansion in Kigali from 2000 to 2024** using Google Earth Engine and Landsat satellite imagery. The **Normalized Difference Built-up Index (NDBI)** is calculated from dry-season Landsat imagery for the years 2000, 2010, and 2024. Cloud masking and median compositing are applied to improve image quality, after which pixels are classified into built-up and non-built-up areas based on an NDBI threshold of zero. The built-up area is calculated in square kilometres for each selected year and presented through classified maps and a comparative bar chart. A time-lapse GIF is also produced to visually demonstrate the spatial expansion of built-up areas over the study period.

Overall, the project demonstrates how **GIS and remote sensing can be integrated to understand infrastructure accessibility, identify potentially underserved communities, and monitor urban development over time**. The findings can support evidence-based urban planning, infrastructure investment, environmental management, and sustainable development decision-making in Kigali.
